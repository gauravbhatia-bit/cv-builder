The CV says what he did. This file says **what it was like**, so a letter can carry a person
instead of a list. Everything here came from the career walkthrough on 30 Oct 2026.

## How to build a letter

### 1. Read two things, not one

A CV answers the JD. A letter has to answer the JD **and the company**. Before writing, spend five
minutes on the company itself — what they sell, who to, what stage they are at, what they say about
themselves — and write one honest sentence about why *this* company and not a competitor. If that
sentence cannot be written truthfully, the letter will read as a template no matter how good the
rest is, and the application is probably not worth sending.

### 2. Pick exactly two stories

One **technical or analytical** (proves he can do the job), one **human or commercial** (proves what
he is like to work with). Two stories told properly beat five mentioned in passing. Pick them from
the bank below using `RULES.md` §10.

### 3. Structure

Four paragraphs, under one page, ~300 words.

| Paragraph | Job |
|---|---|
| **1 — The hook** | Why this company, specifically. The honest sentence from step 1. Never "I am writing to apply for…". |
| **2 — The proof** | Story one, told with the number in it. This is the paragraph that gets him the interview. |
| **3 — The person** | Story two. What it says about how he works. Tie it to something the JD actually asked for. |
| **4 — The close** | Available from December 2026, in Berlin, with the permit. One line, no pleading. |

### 4. Guardrails

- **Never claim what he did not do.** The limits are written into each story below. The precise
  version is always more persuasive than the inflated one.
- **No "passionate", "motivated", "team player", "results-driven", "dynamic environment".**
- **German level is A2 and improving.** If the JD wants German, say the level plainly and move on.
  Do not hide it and do not overstate it.
- **Do not repeat the CV.** The letter exists to say the thing the CV format cannot hold.
- **One number per paragraph, maximum.** A letter dense with figures reads like a second CV.
- **German applications are formal.** "Dear Ms/Mr <surname>" where the name is known, "Dear Hiring
  Team" where it is not. Never the first name alone.

### S1 · The Lithuania export order

**The facts** — At Troika International, Gaurav was handed an export order end to end: 500 meditation
chairs to a client in Lithuania, costed at ₹5,500 a unit, ₹2.75M in total. He went to the
manufacturer in person to source and book it. The deal was close to collapsing because the delivery
window was shorter than sea cargo allowed. He moved the shipment to air freight — more expensive per
unit — and renegotiated terms with the client so the order survived at the new cost. It delivered
inside the original window.

**What it proves** — Commercial judgement under a deadline. He did not report that the order was
going to be late; he changed the economics and sold the change. He has carried a real consequence,
which almost no junior analyst has.

**Reach for it when** the JD mentions ownership, stakeholders, negotiation, supply chain, logistics,
international trade, deadline pressure, or "comfortable with ambiguity". It is the strongest story
in this file for any commercial, pricing, operations or trade role.

**The limit** — He was *given* the project. He did not win the client or originate the deal. Write
"I was handed this order end to end", never "I won this deal". Do not name the client.

> *Starting point.* At Troika I was given a single export order to run from end to end — 500 units
> to a client in Lithuania, ₹2.75M, from sourcing at the manufacturer through to delivery. Partway
> in it became clear the agreed delivery window was shorter than sea freight could meet, and the
> order was about to fall through. Rather than report the slip, I priced the alternative, moved the
> shipment to air freight and renegotiated terms with the client so the economics still worked for
> both sides. It landed inside the original window. That is the part of analysis I care about —
> the decision at the end of it, not the report.

### S2 · Eight years running a business

**The facts** — Bhatia Traders, Nov 2015 to Jan 2024. Purchasing, pricing, stock, supplier terms
across 20+ suppliers, and the P&L. Analysis-led changes cut excess-inventory cost by 10% and
identified a ~15% stockout-reduction potential.

**What it proves** — He has been the person who decides, not the person who advises the person who
decides. When he models price elasticity or demand, he knows what a wrong answer costs because he
has paid for one.

**Reach for it when** the JD is commercial, pricing, category, supply chain, or any role where the
analysis has a budget attached. Also whenever a JD says "business acumen" or "stakeholder-facing".

**The limit** — It is a trading business in India, not a corporate P&L. Scale is modest and he has
not been asked for revenue figures. Lead with the decisions he owned, not the size.

> *Starting point.* For eight years I ran a trading business — what to buy, what to charge, how much
> to hold and on what terms. The analysis I did there was never academic; a bad stocking call showed
> up in cash the same month. That is the lens I bring to pricing work now: a model is only useful if
> someone can act on it on Monday.

### S3 · Three ventures alongside

**The facts** — Running concurrently with Bhatia Traders: **Guru Disha Home Tuitions**, a marketplace
matching local home tutors to families; **Shopify e-commerce stores**, built to learn online retail;
and **Pandit Parantha King**, a restaurant he managed.

**What it proves** — He builds things from nothing, repeatedly, by choice. Three different business
models — marketplace, e-commerce, hospitality — each started because he wanted to understand it.

**Reach for it when** the JD is at a startup or scale-up, mentions 0-to-1, ownership, "wearing many
hats", generalist, or founding/early-team. Also for e-commerce companies, where the Shopify stores
make the domain interest real rather than claimed.

**The limit** — These were side ventures, not funded startups. Do not imply scale or exits. "I built
and ran three small businesses alongside" is accurate and sufficient.

> *Starting point.* Alongside the trading business I kept starting things — a marketplace matching
> home tutors to families in my area, a few Shopify stores to learn how online retail actually works,
> and a restaurant. None of them were large. All of them taught me something I could not have read,
> and the e-commerce work is why the pricing and segmentation problems you are hiring for are
> familiar rather than new to me.

### S4 · The A/B test he reported as negative

**The facts** — At KnowGuard AI, a paired A/B test on AWS comparing a fine-tuned model against the
review arm. Result: +0.0318 AUPRC, 95% CI 0.022–0.0418. Statistically positive. But he had set a
0.05 practical-effect threshold **before** running it, so he reported the result as not worth
shipping.

**What it proves** — Intellectual honesty under pressure to produce a win, and that he understands
the difference between statistical and practical significance. This is a character story wearing a
technical costume, and it is the single most valuable paragraph he has for any role that cares
about rigour.

**Reach for it when** the JD mentions experimentation, A/B testing, causal inference, data-driven
decisions, statistics, ML evaluation, or research. Use it for every Data Scientist application.

**The limit** — It is an internship. The word stays. Confirm with KnowGuard before quoting the
figure to one of their competitors. Do not round 0.0318 to 0.03 — the precision is the point.

> *Starting point.* The result I am proudest of this year is one I recommended against shipping. I
> ran a paired A/B test comparing a fine-tuned model to our review arm; it came back positive,
> +0.0318 AUPRC with a 95% CI of 0.022 to 0.0418. But we had agreed a 0.05 practical-effect
> threshold before the test, and the interval sat below it. Reporting a statistically significant
> result as commercially insignificant is uncomfortable, and it is exactly what the threshold was
> set in advance to protect.

### S5 · Teaching himself the thing he now does

**The facts** — He learned SQL on his own, out of interest, while running the business. The first
thing he did with it was analyse his own company's sales data
(`bhatia-traders-sales-analysis`, built Jan 2026). That turned into an MSc in Germany and 19
further repos.

**What it proves** — The career change was not a pivot away from something failing. He had a
functioning business and left it because the analysis had become the part he wanted to do.

**Reach for it when** the JD is junior, mentions "career changer", "non-traditional background",
curiosity or self-direction, or when the letter needs to explain why a Civil Engineering B.Tech and
eight years in trade lead to a data role. It answers "why the change?" before anyone asks.

**The limit** — Self-taught, then formally trained. Do not present the MSc as the start of the
story; the self-teaching came first and is the more interesting half.

> *Starting point.* I came to data the long way round. I taught myself SQL while running a trading
> business, mostly because I was tired of guessing, and the first thing I pointed it at was my own
> sales data. Finding a 15% stockout problem I had been living with for years was the moment I
> decided to do this properly, which is what brought me to a Master's in Germany.

**The facts** — He ended the Troika role in July 2025 deliberately, took a short break, moved to
Germany, and started the MSc at GISMA in September 2025. The post-study permit begins December 2026.

**What it proves** — Deliberate, not displaced. The two-month gap in 2025 is a planned relocation,
not an unexplained one.

**Reach for it when** the letter needs to address the relocation, visa status or availability.
Mostly this belongs in the closing paragraph rather than as a story in its own right.

**The limit** — Keep it to one line. Nobody wants a paragraph about logistics.

> *Starting point.* I am in Berlin, my post-study permit allows full-time work from December 2026,
> and I am available from then.

### S7 · Tally, inventory and the stakeholders

**The facts** — At Troika: pulled operational reports from Tally, monitored inventory and demand
across 700+ vendors and 500+ SKUs, built Power BI dashboards across three divisions, and ran the
communication with the logistics department, vendors, clients and internal stakeholders.

**What it proves** — He has done the unglamorous middle of analytics: getting numbers out of an ERP
nobody likes, making them legible, and then standing in front of the people who have to act on them.

**Reach for it when** the JD mentions ERP, Power BI, reporting, non-technical stakeholders,
inventory, demand planning, or "translating data for the business".

**The limit** — **No SQL here.** The Troika work was Tally and Power BI. His SQL is real and is
proven in the projects; claiming it at Troika is the one thing that would make this story false.

> *Starting point.* Most of my analytics work has been with people who do not want a dashboard, they
> want an answer. At Troika I pulled inventory and demand data out of Tally across 700+ vendors and
> turned it into Power BI reporting for three divisions — and then spent as much time with the
> logistics team and the vendors as I did building it, because a report nobody trusts changes
> nothing.

### S8 · Professional football

**The facts** — 2019–2020, a break inside the Bhatia Traders years to play in the **third tier of the
Indian football league** with Lonestar FC, Jammu & Kashmir.

**What it proves** — Sustained discipline toward something difficult, and the willingness to commit
fully to a thing with a real chance of not working out.

**Reach for it when** — rarely in a letter. This is interview material, and a very good answer to
"tell me about yourself". It reads well in Germany, where semi-professional sport is respected.
Use it in a letter only if the company culture makes it genuinely relevant — a sports brand, an
explicitly team-culture employer, or where the JD asks for something personal.

**The limit** — Third tier, one club, one season-ish. Say exactly that. Do not let it become
"played professional football in India" without the tier.

> *Starting point.* Before any of this I spent a year playing in the third tier of the Indian
> football league. It is the only thing I have done that taught me as much about preparation as
> running a business did.

### S9 · The energy thesis

**The facts** — MSc thesis modelling the 2026 US–Iran conflict's effect on German day-ahead
electricity prices — event study, Granger causality, Chronos and XGBoost forecast arms. Plus six
independent energy repos using OPSD, SMARD, Open-Meteo and EPEX Spot data.

**What it proves** — The German energy domain interest is demonstrated, not asserted. He chose it
repeatedly, unpaid, before anyone was paying him to care.

**Reach for it when** the JD is energy, utilities, CleanTech, grid, trading, or any German company
where the energy transition is part of the business.

**The limit** — The thesis is **in progress** until the viva. Write "Thesis:", never "published"
or "research I completed".

> *Starting point.* I did not arrive at energy through this job posting. My thesis models how the
> 2026 Hormuz disruption moved German day-ahead prices, and six of my repositories before it were
> already built on SMARD and OPSD data. It is the domain I keep choosing when nobody is asking
> me to.
