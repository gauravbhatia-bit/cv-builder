# Voice and Style

Gaurav's own instructions, given 30 Oct 2026. **These override any default writing habit.**
They apply to every CV, every cover letter, and every line of text that reaches an employer.

They do **not** apply to the working files in this repo (`master.md`, `projects.md`, `RULES.md`,
notes in `jd/`). Those are internal and can be written however is clearest.

---

## Always

- **Sound like a real person, not an AI.**
- **No em dashes.** This one is absolute. En dashes in ranges are fine and correct:
  `Aug 2026 – Present`, `0.022–0.0418`, `A–E`. The banned character is the long one used as
  punctuation. Rewrite the sentence instead of swapping in a hyphen; a comma, a colon, a full stop
  or brackets will almost always read better anyway.
- Plain, direct language.
- Cut repetition, filler, buzzwords and overly formal phrasing.
- Preserve his authentic voice. Never exaggerate.
- **Never mention visa, residence or work-permit status.** Availability is a date, nothing more.
- Use **British/European spelling** (analysed, modelled, optimisation, programme) and
  **European date format**. Keep tone suited to employers in Germany and Europe.

## Banned phrases

Never write any of these, in a CV or a letter:

> I am writing to apply · passionate about · dynamic professional · team player · perfect fit ·
> results-driven · motivated · proven track record · leverage · synergy · spearheaded ·
> detail-oriented · think outside the box · hit the ground running · wealth of experience

---

## For a CV

- Professional summary: clear, confident and natural. Not corporate, not robotic.
- Bullets focus on **real achievements, outcomes and transferable skills**, not duties.
- Strong action verbs. Concise.
- **Keep job titles, employers, dates, education and metrics exactly as given.** Never adjust them
  to fit a JD.
- **Invent nothing** — no experience, numbers, tools, achievements or responsibilities.
- Mirror keywords from the job description **only where they genuinely match** his experience.
  See `RULES.md` §6, which says the same thing and is the mechanism.
- Keep the formatting **ATS-friendly**: real text, no tables for content, no text inside images,
  standard section headings.

## For a cover letter

- Warm, specific and human, while still professional.
- **Open naturally**, connecting his background to the role. No "I am writing to apply".
- Say why **this** company and **this** role, using only information he has provided.
- Carry **2–3 relevant achievements** with concrete examples. `COVER-LETTER-BUILDER.md` holds the
  stories; this file governs how they are written.
- Vary sentence length. Conversational-professional.
- **Under 300 words** unless he asks otherwise.
- Close with a confident, polite call to action.

---

## How to check a draft before sending

1. Search the text for the em dash character. There must be zero.
2. Read the opening sentence alone. Would a person say it out loud?
3. Check every banned phrase above.
4. Confirm every number traces to `master.md` or `projects.md`.
5. For a letter: count the words.
6. Read it once more and delete the sentence that is doing the least work. There is always one.
